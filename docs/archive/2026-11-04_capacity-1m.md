## Capacity conclusion

- No measured path crossed the 2x p95 threshold between 1000 and 1000000 rows; every path stayed inside 7 ms p95.
- Queue: 198 of 200 events persisted (56.82 rows/s), 0 duplicate hashes, 0 rows left in processing.
- Highest observed p95 across all paths at 1000000 rows: 5.94 ms (query).
- **Recommended single-database limit: 1M observations fully embedded (~14 GB total footprint).**
  At that size the three list paths and the hybrid query path stay flat (p95 ≤ 6 ms) on the
  measured hardware, and the 1 GB btree/GIN set plus a 7.8 GB HNSW index fit in a 16–32 GB
  instance once `maintenance_work_mem` is raised for rebuilds. Between 1M and ~3M the query
  latency is expected to stay flat (HNSW is logarithmic) but the footprint grows to ~40 GB and
  the HNSW rebuild to ~45 min; beyond ~3M, partition `mem_observations` by `memory_scope_id`
  or move vectors to a dedicated store rather than scaling a single table.
- Operational rules for a 1M-row database:
  - run `ANALYZE` after every bulk ingest (1 s), and let autovacuum handle the heap;
  - schedule the HNSW rebuild as a maintenance-window job with `maintenance_work_mem` ≥ 2 GB,
    or keep it up to date through normal inserts only;
  - keep `VACUUM FULL` out of the routine: at this size a plain `VACUUM (ANALYZE)` is 13 s,
    while a full rewrite is minutes and takes an `ACCESS EXCLUSIVE` lock;
  - budget the ~1 GB btree/GIN set: the two largest non-vector indexes are the V24 token GIN
    (167 MB) and the project/scope composite btrees (122–132 MB), and they are what keep the
    three list paths under 7 ms.
